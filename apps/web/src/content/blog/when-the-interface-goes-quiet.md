_This sample is a reading-experience checklist for exploring the theme._

## Read in a different light

The room changes after sunset. The window becomes a reflection, the lamp becomes the brightest object, and the screen may suddenly feel louder than it did at noon.

Use the theme switch in the header to see this page in both modes. Look at the body copy first, then the quieter details: dates, captions, category labels, and the outline around a quote.

> A comfortable page keeps the small details readable, too.

## Take the keyboard route

Set the pointer aside for a moment. Press <kbd>Tab</kbd> to move through the links and buttons. A visible focus indicator should tell you where you are. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to follow a link or activate the theme button.

- Can you identify the focused control?
- Do the link labels make sense on their own?
- Can you open and close the table of contents?
- Does a long title wrap without hiding its neighbors?

These questions are prompts for exploring a theme, not a claim that a checklist replaces an accessibility review.

## Let the content do the talking

An image description should say what matters in the image. A heading should describe the section. A link should name its destination. Small choices like these help the page make sense in more than one way.

Try the [Markdown sample](/a-small-guide-to-markdown) next. Its code blocks, footnote, and disclosure element give you a few more details to inspect in each theme.

When the interface goes quiet, it has not disappeared. It has simply become easy enough to use that the story can hold your attention again.
