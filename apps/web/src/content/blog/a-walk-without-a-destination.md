_The walk, village, and people in this sample story are invented._

## The path beyond the last house

The road ended at a gate that did not seem to belong to anyone. Beyond it, a path moved between two low walls and disappeared into grass. There was no sign, which made it feel less like a route and more like an invitation.

Mara had packed a notebook, a pear, and a map she did not intend to consult. The morning was cool enough for a jacket and bright enough to make the jacket feel temporary. Somewhere behind the houses, someone was moving empty bottles into a crate.

### What came along

- A small canvas bag
  - A notebook with several blank pages
  - A pencil wrapped in a scrap of paper
- A pear, carefully chosen
- Enough time to take the longer way home

## The bench at the bend

At the first bend stood a bench with an excellent view of almost nothing in particular. A field sloped down toward another field. A tree leaned slightly away from the wind. Two birds took turns occupying the same fence post.

Mara sat long enough to eat the pear. She drew the line of the hill, erased it, then drew a less accurate line that somehow looked more like the place. Beside it she wrote, _the path is only in a hurry when I am_.

> The notebook did not need a complete account of the morning. It needed one detail that would bring the morning back.

## A different way home

On the return walk, the village looked newly arranged. A window she had missed now held a blue jug. The gate cast a striped shadow. The sound of bottles had been replaced by a radio too far away to understand.

Nothing remarkable had happened. That was the detail Mara wanted to keep: a morning could be worth remembering without becoming an event.

She put the map back on the shelf, still folded. The notebook stayed on the table, open to the crooked line of the hill.
