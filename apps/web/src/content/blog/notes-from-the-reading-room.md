_A fictional reading-room visit, written as sample content._

## The chair beside the window

The best chair in the room was not the newest one. Its arms had been polished by years of sleeves, and one leg needed a folded piece of paper to stop it rocking. From that chair, you could see the whole shelf of blue books and a square of sky above the roof opposite.

Someone had left a pencil in the gutter of a book. The point was blunt. The note beside it said only, “Read this again.”

> “What are you looking for?” asked the librarian.
>
> “A sentence I can carry home.”
>
> “Then you probably need to sit down.”

## Three things to notice

1. A good opening can begin with a small, ordinary object.
2. A quoted conversation changes the pace of a paragraph.
3. A useful note does not have to become an essay immediately.

The notebook on the table had three labels. They were not a system so much as a way of finding things later.

<dl>
  <dt><strong>Image</strong></dt>
  <dd>Something the sentence lets you see: a blue shelf, a blunt pencil, a square of sky.</dd>
  <dt><strong>Question</strong></dt>
  <dd>Something the sentence leaves open, inviting you to keep reading.</dd>
  <dt><strong>Echo</strong></dt>
  <dd>A detail that returns later with a slightly different meaning.</dd>
</dl>

## What came home

By the time the light reached the carpet, the notebook held half a page. No grand conclusions, just a few observations and one phrase with a line beneath it: _make room for the second reading_.

The chair still rocked when the paper slipped. The sky had turned the color of the books. It was enough for an afternoon.
