_The conversation in this sample is fictional._

## Bring something unfinished

The invitation was simple: bring a question, a sketch, or a sentence that had not found its ending. No slides. No carefully arranged introduction. Just something that could become more useful after another person had looked at it.

At the table were two cups, a vase with one leaf, and a stack of cards small enough to discourage speeches.

> “What if the notebook had fewer sections?”
>
> “What would you miss?”
>
> “Perhaps that is what we should find out.”

## Keep a record of the useful part

The first card held a list of possibilities. The second held a question. By the third, the group had stopped trying to name the whole project and started deciding what to try on the next page.

| Idea                 | A useful question            | Next small step   |
| :------------------- | :--------------------------- | :---------------- |
| A reading journal    | What do we want to remember? | Save one passage  |
| An illustrated essay | What does the picture add?   | Sketch one scene  |
| A small publication  | Who is the first reader?     | Write one welcome |

<details>
<summary>A note from the edge of the table</summary>
<p>The cards were not sorted by importance. They were sorted by what someone was willing to try before the next conversation.</p>
</details>

## Leave with one thing to try

One person went home to write about a [reading room](/notes-from-the-reading-room). Another began a [palette study](/a-palette-from-the-garden). The last card became the first paragraph of [a page for every kind of story](/a-page-for-every-story).

That was enough. The table did not need to produce a grand plan. It had given three unfinished ideas somewhere to begin.
